# Practical use and commercial assessment

The demonstrated use is an auditable distinction between **throughput recovery**, **gross-receipts recovery** and **destination broadening**. These outcomes can disagree in actual trade records. The accompanying offline viewer implements the distinction and provides exact accounting components, without a predictive model.

## Observable decision problem

An exporter, regional development institution or commodity buyer can be misled by a headline that treats higher physical exports or lower concentration as sufficient evidence of recovery. Georgia's 2024 and 2025 records demonstrate both problems. The evidence establishes that this diagnostic issue exists in the selected case. It does not measure its prevalence across customers or industries.

## Existing provision

Trade-data vendors already offer country, product, shipment, exporter and importer information. Examples identified during the review include:

- [TradeInt's Georgia HS 720230 export page](https://www.tradeint.com/search/georgia/export-data/hs-code/72023000/), advertising shipment fields and related export totals.
- [Volza's Georgia manganese shipment page](https://www.volza.com/p/manganese-ore-or-chromite-ore/export-data/exports-from-georgia/), offering shipment and buyer/supplier records.
- [UN Comtrade](https://comtrade.un.org/), the public statistical source used here.

These are evidence that trade intelligence is an existing product category, not evidence that this observatory has no competitors. Published data pages can have different scopes, revisions or aggregation rules; their figures are not substituted for the archived research extraction.

## Feasible product extension

A small analytics service could automate unit/field validation, require a named benchmark, show both weight and receipts concentration, and separate expansion of smaller markets from contraction of large ones. A report would retain the source query, revision date and exact arithmetic behind each label. Candidate customers are exporter finance/strategy teams, procurement risk teams and regional development agencies. Access to firm-level orders, costs and inventories would be needed before offering firm-level continuity assessments.

The software supplied here is a working prototype for retrospective inspection. It is not a tested forecasting service. There is no customer-interview study, measured willingness to pay, TAM estimate or patent claim. The open accounting methods themselves are established. Any commercial differentiation would need to come from dependable data operations, coverage, integrations and a verified decision workflow, not branding an existing index as a new invention.
